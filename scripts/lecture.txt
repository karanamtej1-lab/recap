How memory works|Psychology 101 · Lecture 7
Welcome back, everyone. Today we are talking about memory, and specifically, why you forget almost everything you study the night before an exam. By the end of this lecture, you should be able to explain three things: the forgetting curve, the spacing effect, and retrieval practice. And I promise, if you actually use these ideas, your grades will change.
The forgetting curve|Ebbinghaus, 1885
Let's start with a question. How much of today's lecture do you think you will remember tomorrow? Here's the uncomfortable answer. In the eighteen eighties, a German psychologist named Hermann Ebbinghaus memorized thousands of nonsense syllables and tested himself over time. The forgetting curve is the steep drop in memory that happens right after learning. Within one day, he had lost more than half of what he learned. Within a week, most of it was gone. That curve is not a personal failing. It is how every human brain works.
The spacing effect|Review over days, not hours
So here is the secret that most students never hear. The spacing effect means that reviewing material in several short sessions spread over days beats one long session, even when the total time is exactly the same. Every time you review right before you are about to forget, the curve gets flatter. Memory researchers call this one of the most reliable findings in all of psychology. Cramming feels productive, but it is really the worst way to study, because the memories fade almost as fast as you build them.
Retrieval practice|Test yourself, don't reread
Now, what should you actually do in those study sessions? Most people reread their notes and highlight. That feels easy, and that is exactly the problem. Retrieval practice means pulling information out of your memory, for example with flashcards or practice questions, instead of putting it back in. In one famous study, students who took practice tests remembered about fifty percent more a week later than students who reread the same material. The struggle to remember is what makes the memory stronger.
